Le présent des verbes en -er

Objectif

Say what you do regularly: where you live, work, what you speak and eat.

I.Le système

A regular -er verb is built from two parts. The stem is the infinitive minus -er (parler gives parl-), and the ending is chosen by the subject’s person and number. About nine French verbs in ten work this way, including the ones the language keeps inventing (googler, liker).

Spelling keeps all the endings; speech has dropped three of them. Written French has five different forms here, spoken French only three, so in conversation the subject pronoun does the work the ending no longer does.

II.À l’oral, à l’écrit

élision

je + aime becomes j’aime: before a vowel, je loses its e in writing as well as in speech.

liaison

nous‿aimons /nuzɛmɔ̃/, ils‿écoutent /ilzekut/: the silent s of the pronoun is pronounced as z when a vowel follows.

ancrage

The -s in English she speaks is the last audible piece of an ending system English mostly lost. French went halfway: the endings survive on paper, but most of them went quiet.

III.Exemples

Je travaille dans un laboratoire.
I work in a laboratory.
